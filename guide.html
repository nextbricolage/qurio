User Guide

Last updated: 9 November 2026

Qurio adds a Stack-Overflow-style Q&A and FAQ experience to Atlassian Confluence Cloud: ask and answer questions, mark an accepted answer, vote, organize with tags, and embed a question list on any page. This guide walks through everything the app does. Qurio runs 100% on Atlassian Forge — there is nothing to install on a server and no account to create beyond installing the app from the Marketplace.

1. Install & first steps

  1. Install Qurio on your site from the Atlassian Marketplace and approve the requested permissions.
  2. Anyone who administers the Confluence site is automatically a Qurio moderator — there is no "claim" step, so you have full control immediately after install.
  3. Open Qurio either from the global page or by embedding the macro on a page (see the next section).

2. The two ways to view Q&A

The global page

Qurio adds a "Qurio Q&A" global page to Confluence (reachable from the Apps menu). It shows the full question list across your instance — the best place for a central Q&A hub.

The embeddable macro

On any Confluence page, edit the page and insert the "Qurio Q&A / FAQ" macro (type /Qurio in the editor). The macro renders a question list inline, scoped and styled by its per-embed settings (section 9). Use it to put a focused FAQ or a space's Q&A directly where readers are.

The macro's display settings (gear icon) appear only while you are editing the page. In normal (view) mode readers just see the question list plus the "?" help button.

3. Asking a question

  1. Click Ask a question.
  2. Enter a title and a body, and optionally add up to 8 tags to categorize it.
  3. Submit. Your question appears at the top of the list as "open".

You are automatically set to follow your own question, so you are notified when someone answers it. Questions and answers have generous length limits (titles up to 300 characters; bodies up to 30,000) — enough for detailed posts while keeping input sane.

4. Answering & accepting

Open a question and use Post an answer to reply. A question can have many answers.

The question's author (or any moderator) can mark one answer as the accepted answer with the check mark. The accepted answer is highlighted and the question's status becomes "answered". Accepting is a toggle — you can switch it to a different answer or clear it. The answer's author is notified when their answer is accepted.

5. Voting

Give questions and answers a thumbs up or down to surface the most useful content. Voting is idempotent (clicking again clears your vote) and you cannot vote on your own posts. A moderator can turn voting off instance-wide in the settings (section 10).

6. Tags, search & featured FAQs

  • Tags: add tags when asking; then filter the list by a tag. The tag list is derived live from the questions that actually use a tag, so it never shows orphans.
  • Search: full-text search across question titles, bodies and answers, with sorting (newest, most voted, etc.).
  • Featured / FAQ: a moderator can pin a question as featured. Featured questions are surfaced first — ideal for building a curated FAQ at the top of a list or macro.
  • Status: questions are "open", "answered" (an answer was accepted) or "closed". A question's author or a moderator can change the status; a closed question accepts no new answers.

7. Notifications & following

Qurio keeps lightweight in-app notifications (the bell icon). Authors and answerers are auto-followed; anyone else can follow a question to be notified of new activity, or unfollow to stop. You are notified when a question you follow gets a new answer, and when your answer is accepted.

8. Stats & experts

Qurio can show a "Your stats" box and an experts leaderboard. Points are awarded Stack-Overflow-style:

ActionPoints
Your answer is accepted+10
An upvote on your answer+2 each
You ask a question+1

The leaderboard shows the top contributors; anonymized (erased) users never appear. Both boxes are optional per macro (section 9).

9. Configuring an embedded macro

While editing a page, select the Qurio macro and open its settings (gear). Each embed is configured independently:

SettingWhat it does
HeadingA custom title shown above the list.
ScopeShow questions from this space only, or from all spaces.
Tag filterLimit the embed to a single tag (e.g. a focused FAQ).
Display modeFull list, or a compact teaser showing the first N questions.
Teaser countHow many questions the teaser shows.
Show stats / Show expertsToggle the "Your stats" box and experts leaderboard for this embed.
Full-view linkAn optional link from a teaser to the full list.
These are display-only settings for that one embed. They never change who may ask/answer or whether voting is on — those are instance-wide and moderator-only (next section).

10. Moderators & permissions

Open the instance settings (available to moderators). Here you control, for the whole instance:

  • Voting: on or off.
  • Who can ask and who can answer: Everyone, Moderators only, or members of a specific Confluence group.

Who is a moderator? Every Confluence site administrator is automatically a moderator, and moderators can add or remove additional moderators explicitly. Moderators can accept answers on any question, edit or delete any post, feature questions, change settings, and erase a user's data on their behalf.

11. Managing & deleting your data

  • Delete a post: the author (or a moderator) can delete a question or answer. Deleting a question also removes its answers and votes.
  • Delete all my data: open the in-app Help dialog (the "?" button) and choose to erase your data. Your authored posts are anonymized (kept so other threads stay readable, but no longer linked to you), and your votes, notifications and follows are removed.

See the Data Security & Privacy Statement and Privacy Policy for full details on data handling, retention and your rights.
